Pathfinder Beginner Box: Secrets of the Unlit Star Review

September 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

Howdy everyone and welcome to a review of the Pathfinder Second Edition Beginner Box: Secrets of the Unlit Star! It is designed to provide a fairly complete starter package for the TTRPG curious, and comes with everything you need to run your first few sessions, along with a sample adventure, the titular Secrets of the Unllit Star.

Before continuing, we would like to thank Pathfinder publisher Paizo for providing a copy of this box for review.

Secrets of the Unlit Star Box - Pathfinder Second Edition. Image Credit: Peri

Contents

The box, as mentioned above, contains all the essentials to run both the included adventure and a variety of goodies on top of that. You get two rulebooks, one for everyone and one for the Game Master specifically, a large double sided map for the included adventure, a set of 4 pre-generated characters and 6 blank sheets to make your own characters using the included character creation rules, a set of 4 quick reference cards that provide a great summation of basic actions, rules, and conditions, a huge heap of cardboard standees (containing all the pre-generated characters, every possibly ancestry and class combination, and both the monsters contained within the starting adventure and, surprisingly to me, quite a few extra monsters on top of that!), and, finally, a set of standard polyhedral dice which I personally find oddly appealing. The colors are very pleasant and the text is huge, bright, and readable. As someone with a long-standing hatred of difficult to read, darkly colored dice, this is hugely appreciated.

Secrets of the Unlit Star box contents. Look at those dice, those are some really edible looking dice, I love them. Credit: Peri

This is a solid amount of assorted RPG components, and there is enough here to cover quite a few sessions. Nothing feels cheap, and it all packs up nicely. This is very much a box targeted at those without pre-existing collections of RPG stuff, and I mean that in a good way.

I am particularly happy with the decision to go for standees over miniatures in a starter product like this. Miniatures are nice, but painting them is an entire other hobby and you tend to get far fewer of them for the cost. The ability to run a wider range of encounters for less cost is very much appreciated, and they pack up much tighter, reducing the amount of things you need to haul to the local game shop or store on a shelf.

Hero’s Handbook

The rules included in the Hero’s Handbook cover a limited slice of the overall Pathfinder Second Edition ruleset. The character rules cover 5 ancestries (Human, Orc, Elf, Halfling, Dwarf) and 4 Classes (Bard, Monk, Sorcerer, and Barbarian), a small scattering of the game’s many feats, and leveling rules to cover levels 1-3. The rest of the rulebook covers the core player facing systems of Pathfinder Second Edition. In addition, a small choose your own adventure style solo adventure is included, which is much appreciated. 

The rules are competently laid out and presented, with clear and easy to understand language throughout. I am a particularly huge fan of the character creation summary. The lettering of where to look on your sheet for a given value or score is used consistently throughout the book. The dice are color coded every time they appear, which helps hugely when you are introducing players who have never played RPGs. Being able to consistently say “The D20 is the red one” whenever a player asks which to roll is nice and as someone who has introduced a lot of new players to this game I am actively embarrassed that I never thought of doing this myself. 

The included pre-generated character sheets came with these nice art splash pages in small booklets, something I had never seen before, but that was very nice! Credit: Peri

The included classes are all fairly easy to understand, with decent diversity in roles and gameplay styles. The Bard is a support-focused generalist, the Sorcerer fights at range with powerful magic, the Monk is fast and makes a lot of attacks, and the Barbarian is tough and hits people really, really hard. These classes roughly map onto the classic “Fighter, Mage, Thief, Priest” ISO standard RPG party, though I do personally find it a bit odd that they didn’t just include the actual Fighter, Wizard, Rogue, and Cleric classes. Considering the classic fantasy theming in both the available ancestries and the included adventure I would have expected those classic classes, but this isn’t an actual issue, just an interesting note.

I think that the extremely limited character creation rules and options are a strength of this box on the whole though, as the full Pathfinder Second Edition character building ruleset can be extremely overwhelming, particularly to new players. The more things you have to pick through before you can start playing, the higher your chances of either doing something wrong or giving up on it before you start. It is also helpful for the GM to know exactly what they are working with when it comes to party capabilities, and having those limited options means you don’t need to keep too many things in mind at any given time.

Game Master’s Guide

The Game Masters Guide covers all of the non-player facing rules, monster statblocks, a small amount of lore on Ustalav and the town of Grayce, and the included adventure module. I’ll cover the adventure module, Secrets of the Unlit Star, in its own section below. 

To start off, the rules layout and language remains clear and usable throughout this book. I didn’t see a single rule or bit of text that caused me any confusion during my initial read through. I am an experienced GM, but even setting that aside I don’t think anything in here would cause any real problems for a new GM. Considering the width and depth of Pathfinder as a game system, that is a difficult thing to have pulled off. I only had to check the book a handful of times while running the included adventure, and referencing it was easy and clear.

The best part of this whole book though, in my opinion, is the general Game Mastering advice section included towards the start that covers what you should do to start a session, tips on how to organize games, notes on breaks and how to minimize distractions, a section on making sure that one player isn’t hogging the spotlight and to make sure to split your attention evenly, and a range of other concise and very helpful game mastering advice. This section is short at only a couple pages, but 10 year old me could have really used it back in the day. As this is a product targeted at those who have never played RPGs before, this section is going to be very valuable in avoiding some of the common pitfalls that new GMs can run into. While a lot of other RPGs have a “What is an RPG and how do you run one” section, this is one of the most concise and clear I have read, and the overwhelming majority of it is applicable to any game. 

I also want to shout out the amount of quick-reference material provided in this book. The back cover has summations of the most common sorts of checks and rolls, the order of a turn, how you make an attack roll or a save, what cover does, and a few common traits that different sorts of rolls and attacks can have. On the inside of the back cover are other things you will want to reference, though these you will need a bit less often. I particularly appreciate the chart of “What knowledge skill do I roll to identify this monster”, as I have seen that trip up a lot of newer GMs.

The book also has a brief section on designing your own adventures, with both system specific and generic advice on how to construct them. There is a bit of a focus on dungeons in this section, but the advice is good and having a clear list of what your “Budget” is for monsters to construct a reasonable encounter for your players is very much appreciated. Encounter design tends to trip up new GMs, so I appreciate having this section here for those who don’t want to run the included adventure module. I have many horror stories I could share of monsters I intended to be an easy filler fight nearly killing my entire party, and these rules should help new GMs avoid that.

Speaking of monsters, there is a miniature Beasterie included in this book, with nice art for each monster and a concise statblock. The only complaint I have with this section of the book is that I would have loved to have seen something akin to the “Tactics” section that some of the 1st edition Adventure Paths had, calling out what the goal of the monsters is in a fight. When you are new to a given RPG system it can be difficult to intuit how you are supposed to use a given monster statblock, so the omission is notable. That said, whenever monsters appear in the included adventure module, they typically have combat tactics listed, so this is only a problem if you want to run things other than said included adventure module.

Overall I am particularly fond of this book. While most of the book is devoted to the included module or to system specific rules and rolls, those advice sections will help you out no matter what you go on to play, and there were a couple of things in there that were useful even to me with my 18-ish years of experience running games (namely the statement that knowledge skills should be rolled by the GM and the result kept secret from the players, only giving them what their character thinks they can remember without the player knowing if they rolled a 1 or a 20. I had genuinely never thought of that and I am a bit embarrassed about it).

Secrets of the Unlit Star

On to the included adventure module! As a warning, I will be spoiling this adventure a bit here, so if you are interested in this from the player side of things I would skip this section and just know that it's a pretty solid, straightforward starter adventure that your GM will have an easy time running.

I ran this with a group of varying levels of experience. While all had played RPGs before, only one player had any familiarity with Pathfinder 2E as a system. Secrets of the Unlit star is a classic fantasy dungeon crawl, a sunken subterranean temple corrupted by an evil priest full of undead, weird magical creatures, foul gnomes, and a central puzzle mechanic built around finding the various pieces of the titular Unlit Star, a magical artefact MacGuffin. The actual plot of this adventure isn’t particularly likely to surprise you or your players, the Villain is an evil Shadow Priest who wants to take the MacGuffin and use it to do evil shadow priest things. This can be comforting though, and if you are playing with the sorts of extremely new groups that the box is aimed at they won’t have seen this plot before, and vanilla can be a nice flavor even if you are more experienced.

Our brave heroes battle several loathsome Giant Centipedes in what will likely be many player's first RPG combat ever. Credit: Peri

The encounters here cover a wide range of the game’s rules, and the way they are presented I find very interesting. Most of the encounters on the first floor are tutorializing a given aspect of Pathfinder’s ruleset, with a very clear focus. The first fight teaches you and the players how to roll attacks, the second one teaches you about ranged combat, difficult terrain, and movement, the third is about special monster abilities, the fourth covers resistances and immunities, there is an encounter that teaches you about NPC spellcasters, things like that. I have never seen a “starter” adventure module do this sort of “Video Game Tutorial” style structure, and I genuinely really enjoy it. The nature of this module as a pure dungeon crawl helps with that, as the writers could set a specific order for those first few encounters that they would know with 100% certainty which ones you would do in what order.

Despite the nature of the adventure as a pure dungeon crawl, there are still a couple of social encounters, one with the ghost of a dead elf and another with a fungus creature that wants to sell the party things. Said fungus creature is a fun way to replenish your party’s resources, selling some healing items and other magical junk. The module also suggests a couple of opportunities for roleplaying between player characters, though this is, overall, not a particularly social or intrigue centric adventure. That straightforwardness is likely a good thing though considering the new player focus of this box on the whole, and the provided roleplaying opportunities are likely enough to get your players started and accustomed to the concept. 

My only actual structural issue with this adventure module is that the Barbarian pre-constructed character, Amiri, had a tendency when we played through this to just delete entire encounters in 1-2 turns. Some of this was dice luck, but some of the rest of it was just that a lot of the earlier encounters in this module have low HP pools that a Barbarian with a big weapon can fairly consistently deplete in a single attack due to all the bonus damage from Rage. This isn’t really that major of an issue in the grand scheme of things and we still had a great time, but it is something that I did note while playing.

Overall I quite like this module, it ran smoothly and was a nice palette cleanser as a more experienced GM. It can be a lot of fun to run some very classic dungeon crawl-y heroic fantasy from time to time, but I wouldn't recommend buying this entire box just for this adventure. It's a great pairing with all the other contents of the box and it will work pretty well as a first adventure, but it isn’t the sort of thing I would run for a more experienced party who are just new to Pathfinder 2E as a system.

Final Thoughts

In conclusion I highly recommend this box if you are new to roleplaying games as a whole. It has some solid advice, good gameplay aids, and a solid, easy to run first adventure. 10 year old me would have loved the hell out of this box. If you are a more experienced GM and are just curious about Pathfinder 2E as a system, my recommendation comes with a few caveats. A lot of the more general-use RPG things in this box are likely to be things you already have, and if you can stomach the full ruleset you are likely better off with a copy of the core rulebooks and a module of your choice. That said, if you want to dip your toes in for a lower buy-in than all of those books, this is still a solid purchase and will let you know after a couple of sessions if you want to continue with Pathfinder 2E. If your players are more experienced, I would recommend using the rules in this box to design your own adventure, or to build on top of the included module to add a bit more complexity and difficulty.
