Games Industry News Roundup- September 8th, 2026

September 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Here at Tabletop Battles, we know that it’s hard to keep track of all the news happening all the time in the games industry. So much is always going on, all over the place, all the time. With games of all sorts, and their related media, being an informed gamer can feel impossible some days. 

That's why every week, we help you stay in the know by rounding up five of the biggest recent stories in the gaming and gaming-related pop culture sphere in the Games Industry News Roundup. Our trusty news boy, Dan “Swiftblade” Richardson, is right here at the very real and cool TTB newsdesk with the scoop.

PAX Returns to Texas with New Event in 2027

Credit: Penny Arcade/ReedPop

Great news for tabletop gaming fans who live in Texas to start us off this week, as Penny Arcade announced this week that PAX is returning to Texas in 2027 with a new PAX Unplugged event in Houston.

PAX Unplugged, the tabletop gaming cousin of the video-game centric PAX convention, started in 2017, and has since become one of the most popular conventions for all swathes of tabletop gaming, including Roleplaying games, TCGs, and Board Games. In previous years the show was held annually in Philadelphia, but the growing popularity of tabletop gaming has allowed for Penny Arcade to run a second PAX Unplugged annually.

The return to Texas is notable for PAX following the cancellation of PAX South, which was held in San Antonio until it was cancelled following its 2021 event.

"When we made the decision to investigate the feasibility of another Unplugged, we considered a number of major cities factoring in demand from our exhibitor partners to place a tabletop PAX in this region and city infrastructure,” PAX event manager Sean Gonzalez told Gamesindustry.biz. “We were always partial to returning to the South, and after comparing a few options we were happy to find that not only was Houston the best place to launch a second Unplugged, but it was a chance to bring PAX back to the PAX South fans that missed us."

As for the reason Houston was selected, alongside other major considerations such as infrastructure, venue capacity, and airport proximity, PAX organizers cited Houston as an ideal location for its demographics. PAX organizers told Polygon in an interview, “We think the dork population is quite high in Houston.” 

An editorial note here: yes, I know I live in Houston. No, I am not so dorky, such a turbo-dweeb, such a dense specimen of unbelievable nerd, that they’re holding an entire tabletop convention in Houston because of me. Please, allow me what little shreds of dignity I have left.

PAX Unplugged Houston is scheduled to be hosted from June 25th, 2027 to June 27th, 2027 at the George R. Brown Convention Center. Tickets, as well as vendor applications and media passes, are scheduled to go live later this year.  

Saber Interactive Promises No Generative AI in Space Marine 3

Credit: Saber Interactive/Games Workshop

Good news for Space Marine fans as well as AI haters! Despite their recent run ins with generative AI trouble, Saber Interactive recently released a statement refuting the use of any generative AI in any of its licensed games, including the upcoming Space Marine 3.

The news comes from a Gamescom interview with IGN, where Saber COO was approached with the question of generative AI use in future titles, following the controversy surrounding their upcoming title, Rideshare Simulator. 

“There is absolutely no generative AI in Space Marine 3 or Hellraiser or Stuntman or Turok or Jurassic Park, or Wick,” Tim Willits told IGN. “But like we said, we're going to experiment with it. Try it out in Rideshare, see if it works. See if people like it. If they don't like it, we're like, ‘All right.’”

The news is a relief for many Space Marine 3 fans who were worried about the technology making its way to the grim darkness of the far future following a recent controversy with the aforementioned Rideshare Simulator title. Earlier in August, Saber came under heavy fire from both the industry and fans as news came out that the company was replacing writing staff with generative AI on Rideshare Simulator. Saber CEO, Matt Karchs, responded with toxic words against the ex-writer who broke the news, though he would later apologize for what was said.

The use of AI in Space Marine 3 also conflicts with the anti-AI policy currently adopted by Games Workshop, as stated in an earnings report in January.

While no AI is being used in any of Sabers licensed games, Willits is still open to using the tech on other titles, telling IGN “We've always been using AI in some stretch of the imagination, but we as in developers, we don't really know what to do with it. Honestly, we don't. If anyone tells you they do, they don't. And then players, they don't really know what to do with it. So I do believe this is going to happen. Somebody — I don't know who — somebody will come up with a game that is so freaking cool that it will change everyone's opinion and it'll be like, ‘Oh, that's it.’ And then things will settle.”

Space Marine 3 is currently in the middle of development, with no release date yet announced.

Steamforged Resolves Accounting Trouble with British Crown

Credit: Steamforged Games

Its been a wild ride for the team over at Steamforged Games this week. Alongside a meaty two and a half hour preview of showing off upcoming Menoth and Cryx models on Tuesday, a mandatory strike off warning from the British government sent the online community into a frenzy of speculation regarding the financial stability of the company.

The strike off warning, which was issued on September 1st, was issued against SFG by the British government for failure to share its annual account reporting within the allotted window. Once issued a strike off warning, companies have two months to report their accounts or provide an adequate reason for the delay. If the company fails to do so, the company is dissolved and its assets become property of the British Crown. 

Steamforged Games addressed the report in a post on their social media page, writing “We’re aware some of you have seen a First Gazette notice relating to Steamforged Games, and we’re sorry for any concern it’s caused. This is a routine notice Companies House issues when a filing is overdue. Our filing was delayed as a result of the company restructuring work we announced earlier this year and is now being resolved. To be clear, all business activities are continuing as normal.”

Said restructuring happened earlier this summer, as SFG cut around a third of the company staff in a pivot away from board games and crowdfunding while reorganizing around the booming popularity of Warmachine. 

As of this writing, Steamforged appears to have resolved its strike off warning with the government, with the compulsory strike-off action being formally discontinued on September 5th, as "Cause has been shown for why the company should not be struck off the register.”

Hasbro CEO Assumes Interim Control Over Wizards of the Coast

Credit: Wizards of the Coast

With the departure of Wizards of the Coast president John Hight on September 1st, fans of both Magic the Gathering and Dungeons and Dragons were left with the all important question of, “Wait, who’s driving this thing?”

Well, now we know who is, in fact, driving this thing, as Hasbro CEO Chris Cocks will be in charge of Wizards until a new president is selected.

Hight announced he would be leaving the company on July 27th, after having held the role since 2024. His resignation would be formalized on September 1st, giving both Hasbro and Wizards time, in theory, to decide on an interim president while the search for a new president was underway.

No news ever came about who that interim president would be, however, and as September 1st came and went it was unclear just who would be in charge at Wizards. Reporters at Polygon decided to find out by reaching out to Hasbro on the matter, and a representative told them, “On an interim basis, the Wizards leadership team will report directly to CEO Chris Cocks.”

Cocks previously served as the president of Wizards of the Coast from 2016 to 2022, before his promotion to CEO of Wizards parent company, Hasbro. While it’s currently unknown to what extent the Wizards leadership will be reporting directly to Cocks, it does give the Hasbro CEO, at least in the immediate future, an even greater level of direct authority over some of the most profitable IPs in the Hasbro catalogue. 

Life is Strange Team Warn They Lack Funding to Operate Past January 2027

Credit: Square Enix/Don't Nod

Hey, we almost made it through a whole week of the roundup without any troubling news about the video game industry! Hopefully we won’t get hit by a last-minute curveball to ruin our good vibes. Oh, huh, what's that pitcher got there in his hands? Bad news? Uh oh.

Yes, it's big trouble for the team at Don’t Nod, the French developers behind the Life is Strange series, as a recent financial report from the company shows that without a major restructuring or external financing, the studio will lack the funds to continue to operate in January of 2027.

In the financial numbers shared by the studio, gross cash fell from $17.9 million at the end of 2025 to $9.3 million at the end of July 2026. The net operating revenue at Don’t Nod took a similar plunge, dropping over 50% from $16.2 million in 2025 to $7.1 million in 2026.

“The Group's business operates within a profoundly transformed video game market, characterized by highly selective financing and changing conditions for accessing the resources necessary for production development, while commercial success is becoming increasingly difficult to achieve despite the recognized quality of DON'T NOD's original creations,” the leadership team at Don’t Nod writes in their financial report. “Despite the measures already undertaken as part of its performance plan and the cost-cutting actions implemented, these efforts alone are not sufficient to restore DON'T NOD's competitiveness on a sustainable basis.”

Without the funds to keep the doors open themselves, Don’t Nod is faced with two options. The first would be to secure additional external funding, which the studio explored earlier to this year to little success after Tencent, the company's largest stakeholder, declined to provide Don’t Nod with a short term cash infusion.

The other option would be major restructuring at Don’t Nod. 

“With this in mind, DON'T NOD is refocusing its operations in France around a single production line, bringing together the expertise required to launch new projects before the completion of current productions. This organizational initiative is intended to maintain a continuous pipeline of projects and support the Company's long-term growth,” the company writes in the report. “In this context, the transformation project currently under consideration could lead to a workforce adjustment in France that may involve the reduction of up to 90 positions.”

Should the studio receive no additional external capital or fail to save enough money via restructuring, the studio predicts that it will lack the money needed to continue operations past January 31st, 2027, and would be forced to shut down. 

Don’t Nod didn’t mention in its report on what sort of severance packages any employees laid off by the restructuring would get, or if they would receive any at all.
