Conquest First Blood Faction Focus: Hundred Kingdoms

September 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

If you would really rather just play historical wargaming, but can’t find anyone to play with, then look no further! The Hundred Kingdoms are the faction of choice for people who look at a world populated by superhumans, zombies, biological monstrosities, robots and dinosaurs and say to themselves: “No thanks, give me spears and horsies instead.”

The fact that they manage to compete despite being down on several counts of magical bullshit is a testament to the strength of horses and long pointy sticks. And today, we are taking a look at what those horsies and pointy sticks can do for you in Conquest’s skirmish mode! Read on to find out what makes the Hundred Kingdoms tick in First Blood.

Hundred Kingdoms Iconic Artprint – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Faction Overview

As Conquest’s regular joes, the Hundred Kingdoms fulfill all the expectations that come with the job description. They are a bit more numerous than other factions, though not a true horde, unless you want them to be. They rely heavily on ranged Regiments and Cavalry to make up for their disadvantages when fighting mano a mano. And they are heavily drilled and disciplined, with Command Abilities and stuff like flagbearers playing a huge part in their Army Rules. Speaking of which …

Army Rules

This is where I have to admit that I lied to you in the opening paragraph. The Hundred Kingdoms have some magical bullshit, but it is entirely optional for the discerning Historicals player. So optional, in fact, that two of the Hundred Kingdoms sub-factions that dabble in magical stuff hate each other and famously don’t get along. This is due to some business from when the Hundred Kingdoms were founded, following the fall of Hazlia and the Dominion of Man which I won’t get into here.

But let’s not get ahead of ourselves. First off, say it with me now: The Hundred Kingdoms are divided into four keyworded sub-factions for list building and synergies. They are: The knightly Orders, the Feudal nobility, the Imperial remnants and the Theist faith. The Theist and Orders sub-factions are where a lot of the magical elements of the faction are located; as superhuman, divinely infused knights and big walking church golems, respectively, with some faith magic sprinkled throughout.

Orders and Theist Warriors cannot benefit from the opposing side’s Command Abilities or Spells. The Feudal elements of the faction, meanwhile, are entirely malleable and take on an additional sub-faction keyword of your choice in list building.

The rest of the rules are a bit more straightforward and don’t require an historical treatise to understand: Hundred Kingdoms Characters can issue a Command as an Action during their Activation. This crucially means that your Characters can issue two Commands: One during the Command Phase, and another one during their Activation.

Rounding out the army rules is Honored Heraldry, which lets models within 1” of a slain Standard Bearer take up the flag of the fallen and become a Standard Bearer themselves. Fun, flavorful and fairly powerful as long as your units stick close together.

Characters

As one of the two founding factions of Conquest, the Hundred Kingdoms have access to a metric ton of Characters and Heroes, with varying degrees of usefulness. Whichever one you choose to bring along, however, keep in mind that due to the nature of the Hundred Kingdoms Army Rules, your Characters are even more important than they usually are. Having access to two Commands per Round is a huge benefit that you want to plan around.

For a brief reminder, here are the available generic Commands. We will call out Unique Command Abilities when going over the individual Characters:

  • Press Forward: Is the best one of the bunch. When a friendly Warrior begins their second March action within Command Range of a Character that used this Command, they get to move their full March distance instead of having to halve it.
  • Rallying Cry: The other one you will want to use most of the time. All friendly Broken! Warriors within Command Range immediately Rally. In addition, they get to re-roll all Failed Resolve Tests when performing Combat Rally Actions this Round.
  • Duel: More situational, but sometimes very useful: You choose an Enemy Character in Command Range and within Line of Sight. They choose whether to Accept or Decline the Duel:

    • If they Accept, you place your Character within Engagement Range of their Character by the shortest possible distance. Only those Characters can affect each other until the Round ends.
    • If they Decline, the Command Ability ends, the Enemy Character becomes Broken! and can not use Command Abilities this Round. Characters that are already Broken! can not decline Duels.

Chapter Mage

Hundred Kingdoms: Chapter Mage – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Surprise! It’s one more magical thing. Though compared to, say, the Sorcerer Kings, the Chapter Mage is not much to write home about. Think of her more as a mage from a low magic setting. Still very powerful, but on equal footing to a bloke with a crossbow, which she fittingly brings along as her Retinue option!

Outside of that, she has a fairly frail statline, as befits a nerd, with Evasion and Resolve 2 and 1 Attack at Clash 2. Her raison d’être is of course her spellcasting, which she is thankfully pretty good at! Spellcaster 3 is about average, but Re-Rolling Attunement Rolls of “6” is pretty good. Once you’re casting, you have the option of choosing between three spells:

  • Elemental Bolt (1 Essence) is your standard nuke. It comes in as a Unique Volley Action with Barrage (2) at 12” with Torrential Fire. A different Special Rule that is more broadly useful would have been nice here, but preventing Enemy Warriors from using their Evasion Characteristic can and will come up sometimes.
  • Warding Glyphs (2 Essence) is anti-ranged tech and fairly useful at that. It gives all Friendly Warriors within her Command Range (a mere 5”, it must be said) the Pavise Special Rule. This means that while unengaged, they each gain +1 Defense. A very strong buff when it matters, but somewhat situational.
  • Enchant (1 Essence) saves the Chapter Mage’s spellbook from being underwhelming. Each time a Friendly Warrior within Command Range Rolls for a Clash or Volley Action, they get to re-roll a single dice. This is potentially massive over the course of a game, especially with a large ranged Regiment accompanying the Chapter Mage.

As it stands, the Chapter Mage is more suited toward being a Hero choice – which she can also slot in as – rather than your Warlord. Unless you really want her Mercenary Crossbowmen Retinue, her 80 points are better spent there with a more impactful Warlord to steer your list.

Crusading Noble Lord

Hundred Kingdoms: Crusading Noble Lord – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Speaking of impact, the Crusading Noble Lord (a variant of the vanilla Noble Lord, which we will get to later), is an absolute sledgehammer. 3 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 2 and Overwhelm is a very respectable statline that can tussle with some of the more powerful martial Characters out there.

Defensively, he is no slouch either at Defense 3 and Resolve 4 with 4 Wounds – and remember, the Hundred Kingdoms like Standard Bearers, so high Resolve is even better here than it is in other factions. When walking all over opposing units, he has some added mobility from Unyielding as well as a Special Rule that lets him move up to half his March Characteristic (5, so 2.5”) as a Draw Event when Engaged with an Enemy Warrior.

The rest of his kit centers, unsurprisingly, on the conferring of buffs on Theist Warriors. Within his 6” Command Range he always hands out Fearsome, while his Command Abilities let you choose between Indomitable and Impact +1 – or you can forgo an Action with your Army Rule to get both! A very tight package for 110 points, the Crusading Noble Lord is a very blunt Character and a no-brainer for Infantry Theist lists.

Errant of the Order of the Shield

Hundred Kingdoms: Errant of the Order of the Shield – Credit: Para Bellum Games

The Errant of the Order of the Shield is your second pick for Warlords that can double as Heroes and he is sadly also better served as second fiddle to a more impactful Character. He is fairly well endowed with stats, lacking only a bit on defense – Defense 2 with the Shield Special Rule; Resolve 3 and 3 Wounds with 3 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 1 and March 6. There is a lot to like here, but the rest of his abilities are a little too niche for my tastes.

He is mainly centered around Militia and Militia Bowmen, giving them Bravery in his 5” Command Range. This is cute, but Rallying as a Draw Event when you have the worst statline in the game, or close to it, is just not impactful enough.

The rest of his Command Abilities are focussed around buffing himself, with your choice between +1 Defense and Tenacious or Cleave +2 – or both if you utilize your Army Rule. I like this a lot better, as it makes the case for playing him without Militia, but it’s still not enough to warrant his spot as a Warlord. Play him as a frontline haymaker, and he will most likely make his points back, since he is so cheap at just 90.

Imperial Officer

Hundred Kingdoms: Imperial Officer – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Take a look at what 90 points can give you in a Warlord, however. The Imperial Officer is a great force multiplier for any army list that leans Imperial. He has two Special Rules, one lets Friendly Imperial Warriors move through each other – invaluable when you are running large blobs of Infantry – and the other offers you the option of letting a Warrior within Command Range perform a Clash Action for free, basically having it act as a proxy for the Officer.

This is all just gravy on top of his great Command Abilities, however, which he can deploy very reliably with his 7” Command Range:

  • Combined Arms gives Imperial Infantry Warriors within Command Range the Phalanx Special Rule – a great offensive tool when you are set up with the proper positioning.
  • Hold Your Ground! gives Tenacious for a Resolve boost when Engaging multiple Enemy Warriors, which synergizes beautifully with the Hundred Kingdoms Army Rule as well as Combined Arms.
  • On My Mark is the wildcard which you won’t always use, but it is still very nice to have. It gives Friendly Imperial Warriors within Command Range the Rapid Volley Special Rule, which inflicts additional Hits on Hit Rolls of “1” during Volley Actions.

Set him up behind a large Infantry wall with a ranged unit nearby, and you’ll be laughing. An absolute steal at 90 points, and he unlocks the Steel Legion as a Mainstay option. They’re not great on their own, but become respectable alongside him. A match made in heaven if ever you’ve seen one!

Mounted Noble Lord

The second variant of the baseline Noble Lord, the Mounted Noble Lord is exactly what it says on the tin, a Noble Lord on a horse. Being on a horse gives you Impact Hits (2, to be precise), a phenomenal March value of 8, as well as Brutal. In addition, the Mounted Noble Lord also has Wedge, which lets him Re-Roll Impact Hit Rolls of “6” and is quite formidable during regular Clashes at 3 Attacks with Clash 3.

Defensively, he shines as well with Defense and Resolve 3, as well as the Shield Special Rule and 5 Wounds to spare. This all adds to a fine statline, but what puts him over the top are his Command Abilities.

  • To Victory! gives Friendly Feudal Warriors in his 6” Command Range Inspired!
  • Valiant Charge allows the Mounted Noble Lord to perform a free Clash Action after charging. This doesn’t quite work with the Hundred Kingdoms Army Rule, which is frustrating, but it is still very good to get to Move, Charge and Clash all in one sitting, since the Mounted Noble Lords Clash Action will also benefit from the Brutal debuff.

At 120 points, the Mounted Noble Lord is the second most expensive Character, and he really wants you to bring a Retinue of Household Knights to compliment him, which can get expensive fast. But I do really like the straightforward offensive power he offers. Just make sure that you can take out or neuter whatever he charges before you commit and he will be a major asset.

Noble Lord

Hundred Kingdoms: Noble Lord – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Finally, there is the regular Noble Lord. 100 points for a March 5 Character is asking a lot, but with smart play, he can justify his points cost. Even before that, though, his statline is pretty decent: 4 Attacks at Clash 3 with Flawless Strikes is very tasty, while Defense and Resolve 3 with 4 Wounds and Parry is also nothing to sneeze at.

Finally he has a Special Rule that has all Friendly Warriors within Command Range Rally when he destroys an Enemy during a Clash action and two Command Abilities:

  • Dig Your Heels gives Feudal Warriors within Command Range the Bastion Special Rule, which is okay as is but very good on his Retinue of choice, the Household Guard
  • Press the Attack gives Friendly Feudal Warriors within Command Range the Opportunists Special Rule, which is potentially very strong with the right positioning.

All in all, I like the Noble Lord. He plays a very honest game and rewards good fundamentals. Plan your game accordingly, position smart and don’t overcommit and a large bubble of Household Guard with an embedded Noble Lord on foot can get very scary if your opponent has nothing to punch through. Just make sure to support your flanks, and you will be eating well.

Priory Commander

Nestled deep within Para Bellum's inscrutable rules layout, you get the choice between your Priory Commander hailing from the Order of the Crimson Tower or the Order of the Sealed Temple, with the corresponding choice being added as a Retinue choice.

Both variants share a stat baseline that is very nice to look upon: March 7, 4 Attacks at Clash 3, Defense and Resolve 4(!) as well as 6 Wounds and Evasion 2. Both versions also feature Cleave 1, Impact 2 and +1 Clash when engaged in a Duel, as well as the same set of two Command Abilities:

  • Reform! lets your Order Cavalry Warriors in Command Range (6”) Disengage without becoming Broken! for added mobility.
  • Bellicosus gives your Order Cavalry Trample for extra Attacks against Warriors of smaller Size.

Outside of modifying your Retinue, the choice between Crimson Tower and Sealed Temple is down to the specialized combat role you’d like the Priory Commander to perform:

  • The Crimson Tower Priory Commander serves as shock Cavalry with Brutal, Fearless, and Terrifying while he is Wounded, to match his Retinue unit.
  • The Sealed Temple Priory Commander offers more finesse and mobility, with Flawless Strikes, Overrun and Peerless Precision (basically Flawless Strikes for Impact Hits).

For 140 points he is not cheap, and those added costs for Standard Bearers in most of your units add up, but he offers a lot in return. The Orders lean elite, so keep in mind that one Priory Commander with a full Retinue of his chosen Order Cavalry is probably all you’re going to be able to afford.

But if you pad the rest of your list with Feudal units to act as a buffer so that you don’t get outplayed on the objectives, he offers you an incredible hammer unit that will put the hurt on all but the most defensive Enemies.

Theist Priest

Hundred Kingdoms: Theist Priest – Credit: Para Bellum Games

If you want to cast spells and sling fists, the Theist Priest is there for you. He has very respectable damage in melee for a caster, at 2 Attacks with Clash 3 and Cleave 1, and can take some damage at Defense 2, Resolve 4 and 3 Wounds.

He also has Blessed to nudge his Damage and his Defense some, as well as Spellcaster 3 and a Special Rule that converts Slain Friendly Theist Warriors into Essence.

That’s not all though, as he offers a very good selection of spells for melee grinding:

  • Consecrate (2 Essence) has every Enemy Warrior in his 6” Command Range suffer 2 Hits. Enemy Spellcasters suffer 3 Hits instead, but it’s unlikely for this to come up much. A very good way for the Theist Priest to chew through weaker units or soften up tough opponents.
  • Divine Strength (2 Essence) gives Friendly Theist Warriors within Command Range Cleave +1. Good, straightforward and almost always useful.
  • Holy Fervor (1 Essence) gives Friendly Warriors +1 Attack within Command Range. Very good, but made even better by the fact that it applies to all Warriors, not just Theist Warriors.

Unsurprisingly, the Theist Priest mainly synergizes with Theist units. As it stands you have two options for that: Crusaders and Sicarii, with the latter being unlocked as a Mainstay unit as the Theist Priests Retinue.

If you don’t mind the somewhat monotonous list building, you will see the Theist Priest put in a lot of work – and the option of conscripting some Feudal Warriors to your crusade is always there for some added variety.

Heroes

And so we come to the Heroes! As the second oldest faction, the Hundred Kingdoms have a lot of random bibs and bobs that got re-imagined as Heroes for First Blood, so the selection here runs almost as deep as the Character pool. Let’s dive in!

Armsmaster

Hundred Kingdoms: Armsmaster – Credit: Para Bellum Games

A hybrid bodyguard and Infantry officer, the Armsmaster clocks in at a cool 75 points. For that, you get an above-average statline of 3s across the board, March 5, Flurry and three Special Rules.

One being Bodyguard, which lets the Armsmaster tank Wounds for any Character within 1”. The other, the perfectly named Defense of Lord and Liege, lets the Armsmaster teleport to any Engaged Friendly Character in Command Range (5”) like he is an action hero.

This is all fine and flavorful, and very nice to have in a pinch, but I prefer just using his Command Ability to give Friendly Feudal Infantry Warriors in Command Range Flurry. Very straightforward and likely what you are buying him for.

A big block of Household Guard with an Armsmaster and a Noble Lord is very strong offensively, fairly reliably safe against all but the smashiest of hammers, and has an added layer of insurance with the Bodyguard package. A very flavorful core for a Feudal list that should be considered whenever you’re planning to footslog.

Chapter Mage

This is your scheduled reminder, since the Character portion of this Faction Focus runs so long, that the Chapter Mage can and should be played as a Hero.

Drillmaster

Hundred Kingdoms: Drillmaster – Credit: Para Bellum Games

The Drillmaster is a very straightforward buff piece for Imperial Infantry. His statline is alright: 2 Attacks at Clash 2, Cleave 1, Defense 3 and 3 Wounds, but with only Resolve 2, but his main point are his Abilities, of which he has 2.

Double Time is a Unique Out of Combat Action that has Friendly Imperial Warriors within Command Range (5”) move up to half their March Characteristic for free. A very welcome ability for Imperial lists, which likely consist of large blocks of Infantry. This alone would likely have you consider running one Drillmaster in most lists, perhaps even 2.

But there’s more! His Unique Command Ability Combat Drills gives all Friendly Imperial Warriors within Command Range Relentless Blows for extra Attacks on Hit Rolls of “1”. If you position yourself well to outnumber Enemy Warriors for extra attacks, you will dramatically increase your damage potential with more attempts at Rolling “1”s. A very nice sweetener on an already decent deal. A steal at a mere 70 points.

Errant of the Order of the Shield

This is another reminder that you can also play the Errant as a Hero. And you probably should, if at all.

Neophyte

The Neophyte is a neat little spellcasting buff bot for Theist units and an absolute wimp in combat. 1 Attack at Clash 1, a pitiful (for a Character) 2 Wounds and Defense 1 are clear indicators that this lad does not want to see frontline action.

Though if he does, his above average Resolve of 3 with the Hundred Kingdoms’ propensity for Standard Bearers let him stick around for a bit longer than you’d expect, mathematically.

What you’re here for is his Spellcasting and his aura. He gives Fearless to Theist Warriors in Command Range (4”), which is okay, but he is a Spellcaster 2 with as many pretty neat spells for Theist lists:

  • Intercession (1 Essence) Heals a Friendly Warrior within Command Range by 1. If they are Theist, they also Rally. This is somewhat unexciting unless you also pay extra for leaders (who come with an added Wound). This is probably not worth it for all the points you’re sinking into it, so the main use case for this spell is to act as a healbot for an Archangel, if you really really don’t want it to die. So, somewhat situational, but the other spell the Neophyte has access to makes up for it.
  • Test of Faith (1 Essence) is a bubble that has all Friendly Theist Warriors Re-Roll Failed Resolve Tests. Just very, very good, especially since, say it with me, the Hundred Kingdoms really like having Standard Bearers around.

If it sounds like I’m a down on the Neophyte, that’s just because I haven’t mentioned the kicker yet. He is hilariously cheap at a cool 45 points. Really nice add-on for any Theist list that has some points to spare.

Null Mage

The Null Mage wants to be an Anti-Spellcaster Hero, but really ends up more of a pretty good force multiplier for all your ranged Units. Whoops!

To wit, he offers 2 Attacks at Clash 2, Barrage (2) at 12” with Volley and Armor Piercing 2 offensively – and some okay durability with Evasion 2 and Resolve 3 defensively. He is, confusingly, also a Spellcaster 2, with 2 Spells to choose from:

  • Nowhere to Hide (2 Essence) has Friendly Imperial Warriors gain Sureshot when performing Volley Actions in the Null Mages Command Ability
  • Witch Hunt (2 Essence) has the Null Mage gain the Deadly Blades and Deadly Shot Special Rules

In general, you’ll likely opt to grant Sureshot to your Warriors to shoot through your Infantry blobs – unless you’re orchestrating a takedown of a key unit. In that case, Deadly Blades and Deadly Shot will come in handy for sure.

The key element of the Null Mage’s kit is his Hunter’s Quarry ability: At the start of the Supremacy Phase, you choose a single Command Card of your opponent’s. The app and PDF say, that all Warriors within this Special Rule get to Re-Roll one dice per Clash and Volley Action.

This is most certainly a typo and instead most likely applies to all Warriors with this Special Rule. Which basically means just the Null Mage and the Hunter Cadre, which he was initially conceived as an Officer for and we will get to later.

Last and least, the Null Mage also moonlights as a Nullifier of mages, with an Aura that increases the Essence cost of Enemy Spells by 1 within his Command Range. It’s okay, but unlikely to come up. Other than that, a great pick for Imperial lists that run Hunter Cadre, and still somewhat affordable at 80 points.

Seasoned Veteran

The Seasoned Veteran, by contrast, is much cheaper and much more straightforward. 55 points nets you a decent melee fighter: 2 Attacks at Clash 3, Defense 2 and Resolve 3 with a Shield and 3 Wounds.

For Special Rules, he offers Cleave 1, a special kind of Counter-Attack that also leaves the Enemy Warrior that attacked him Broken! and a Command Ability that gives Friendly Feudal Warriors in his 5” Command Range Counter-Attack (the regular kind).

This compact package lets you do two things: You either spend a ton of points on a death star, like a blob of Household Guard with a Noble Lord, an Armsmaster and a Seasoned Veteran. This will be very difficult to take down, almost impossible to shift, and quite killy on the attack.

The other option is to slot the Seasoned Veteran into a barebones unit of Men-at-Arms or Household Guard and be very cagey with where you deploy them. The Hundred Kingdoms Army Rules offers you some virtual mobility through extra Commands, so you have a decent amount of say over where and what your units are fighting.

With just a Seasoned Veteran, you can really bully weaker units with his added damage potential and Counter-Attack. It’s probably more efficient to go with the big death star if you’re already spending the extra points, but don’t forget about just putting some juice (and a Character for Commands) on a basic unit.

Servite

Hundred Kingdoms: Servite – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Last in line, we have the Servite. You wouldn’t know it to look at him, but this bookish little nerd is all about mobility. His statline is abysmal, so we’ll skip it in the interest of time.

What makes him worth his 40 points is giving Vanguard to a Feudal unit of your choosing. As an added bonus, he can give Militia or Militia Bowmen +1 Clash as a Command Ability.

He teeters on the edge of usefulness for just the Vanguard and the ability to issue Commands alone, but only really comes into his own when leading Militia of either persuasion. I’m a bit iffy on him, since Militia do so badly on pure stats, but I think it might still be worth a shot to check if the package plays better than it looks.

Mainstay Regiments

Gosh dangit, did we spend a lot of time and digital ink on the Character choices. Time to dive into the Hundred Kingdoms units, which thankfully aren’t that numerous. Right? Right?

No. There are also a ton of units to go over. Hooray! Buckle up, we’re starting off with the Mainstay units, all of which are Feudal and therefore go into any list with no trouble. And we start as we mean to go on, since most of the Hundred Kingdoms Mainstay units are great!

Longbowmen

Hundred Kingdoms: Longbowmen – Credit: Para Bellum Games

We’re starting off strong with the Longbowmen, probably one of the best basic ranged units in the game. They’re cheap enough at 100 points to start with, but slightly expensive to spam with 30 points per additional model. Their stats justify it, though. March 6 is a great starting point to get into position – and remember that you have more leeway to use Press Forward to make all your units even more mobile, if you bring enough Characters.

Everything but their Volley Characteristic is garbage, but it’s not what you’re there for, even if Para Bellum keeps true to tradition with the meme of giving them Cleave 1.

Quick side note on that: Yes, it’s historically accurate. Longbowmen apparently always had heavy sidearms like axes and massive upper body strength from drawing their bows, but why do they then have only 1 Attack at Clash 1? Make up your mind, Para Bellum!

Their main point is of course their ranged damage. Volley 2, Barrage (1) at 14” and Armor Piercing 1 is great for their price point. I like them!

Men-at-Arms

Hunred Kingdoms: Men-at-Arms – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Men-at-Arms are the most basic of basic units. 80 points for four to start, 20 points per additional model, 2 Attacks at Clash 2, Defense and Resolve 2 with a Shield (and, likely, a Standard Bearer). What’s not to like?

You will more than likely include some Men-at-Arms no matter what list you’re playing. Good clean fun!

Militia

Hundred Kingdoms: Militia – Credit: Para Bellum Games

If Men-at-Arms are the platonic ideal of objective chuds, then the Militia are the same for chaff. They are cheap as dirt at 60 points to start off with, and hilariously cheap to bulk out at just 10 points per additional model.

You obviously don’t get much in terms of stats in return, with a statline of 1s across the board, though notably the Militia do have Resolve 2 and a Shield, which is nothing to scoff at. You’ll have enough bodies of these if you want to include them for some lucky Rolls to save a handful of models from being wiped out, further wasting your opponent’s time.

Militia Bowmen

Hundred Kingdoms: Militia Bowmen – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Militia Bowmen are a bit more complicated, since their being ranged sort of tempts you into protecting them, which you don’t want to do. Their ranged output probably isn’t good enough to justify that, with Barrage (1) at 12” with Volley 1.

Run them up the board and spam one or two rounds of arrows at the advancing opponents before tying them up for another turn and hope you get lucky enough to make your points back.

I don’t hate that gambit, since they are fairly cheap at 75 points to start and 15 per additional model, but I would advise you to consider the basic Militia first if you are looking for a screen.

Mounted Squires

Hundred Kingdoms: Mounted Squires – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Just being March 9 for 45 points (and 40 per additional model) makes the Mounted Squires very attractive. To start, you really can’t overvalue mobility in any game – being able to snag objectives in a pinch, help out other units if needed or even just screen a more valuable piece is great.

Add on to that their okay melee stats (2 Attacks at Clash 2 and Impact 1) and their decent defense (Defense and Resolve 2, but with a Shield and 3 Wounds) and you have yourself a very nice little package.

And that is all before you consider that just putting one into your list is enough to unlock a Restricted unit. I really like them! Just make sure you understand that they will not kill anything below their paygrade before running them and you will find yourself surprised by these little bowlcut goofballs.

Restricted Regiments

With the basics out of the way, it’s time that we dove into the more exciting units. Let’s see what the Restricted units of the Hundred Kingdoms bring to the table!

Crusaders

At 100 points to start and 25 per additional model, the Crusaders are a more aggressive upgrade to the Men-at-Arms that they share a kit with. 20 more points upfront and 5 more points per model net you an extra point of Resolve (3) on defense.

Offensively, they get a lot more: Overwhelm, Unyielding and the Crusading Noble Lord’s Special Rule that lets them also move half their March Characteristic when Slaying an Enemy Warrior.

They do exactly what it says on the tin and are a no-brainer with a Crusading Noble Lord who conveniently makes them a Mainstay choice.

Move them up the board, smash through your opponent’s basic Infantry and take up residence in their backline where you will be deceptively hard to shift, owing to Resolve 3 and the Standard Bearer you are likely to bring along.

Just remember to keep the Crusading Noble Lord with them, as they can be a little too pillow-fisted to get things done without him.

Gilded Legion

Hundred Kingdoms: Gilded Legion – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Our first elite choice, the Gilded Legion operates on the porcupine principle. Plonk yourself down somewhere your opponent would rather you didn’t, tank their hits with Defense and Resolve 3 (don’t forget your Standard Bearer), and put out decent damage with 1 Attack at Clash 3 and Flawless Strikes.

“But how?” you ask. “Just having 1 Attack is nowhere near enough to punch through anything that threatens them!” The answer is their Support Special Rule and outnumbering your opponent.

Either bring an extra large blob of them or pair them with something cheap to stand behind their spear wall and thereby give them extra attacks. It’s pricy at 130 points and 40 per additional model, but if you’re building around it, I can see it working out for you.

Household Guard

Hundred Kingdoms: Household Guard – Credit: Para Bellum Games

The Household Guard performs a similar role to the Gilded Legion, which they once again share a kit with, but instead of standing solo in a large blob or with another unit to support, they want to run with a Character or two.

The have the Bodyguard Special Rule to start off with, and still do admirably with a buff or two. They are a tad cheaper than the Gilded Legion at 110 points for four and 30 per additional model, which still gives you respectable damage with 1 Attack at Clash 2 and Cleave 2, and a very solid Defense of 3.

Run them with a Noble Lord at the minimum and probably an Armsmaster for Flurry. It’s costly, but they will be very hard to shift and surprisingly killy, while providing an excellent anchor for devastating Cavalry charges if they get stuck fighting something really scary.

Household Knights

Hundred Kingdoms: Household Knights – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Oh look, a Cavalry unit. The Household Knights are your cheapest option for true shock Cavalry at only 70 points to start and a flat rate of 70 for each further knight.

March 8 is a great place for them to be since it makes landing your Charges fairly easy, which is good, because they rely a lot on Brutal, Impact 2 and Clashing after their Charge to get things done. Without the full monty, they fight like Men-at-Arms with 2 Attacks at Clash 2 and no further multipliers.

They are okay as a mobile screen in a pinch as well, at Defense 3 with a Shield, but in that case they will need another unit to come save them, since they do not do well fighting fairly.

If that sounds like I’m down on them, I’m not! I like them a lot, since you don’t pay for them to fight things fairly. If you can play them well and get their Charges off, they will outperform their points cost any day of the week – a great unit that is cheap because it’s specialized, and not because it’s bad.

Hunter Cadre

Meanwhile, you pay through the nose for the Hunter Cadre, a true hybrid unit. 140 points as a buy-in for a unit with just 1 Wound per model is a tough sell, and bulking them out is also not exactly cheap, since they come in at 45 points per additional model. Let’s see what else they bring to the table.

March 6 is a good start, and the hits keep coming: Volley and Clash 2, 2 Attacks and Barrage (1) at 12” with Armor Piercing 2. They, too, have the weirdly worded Hunter’s Quarry Special Rule for a fun little minigame they can pursue with the Null Mage, and Fearless and Fiend Hunter to boot.

I don’t hate them, and they are certainly playable. but they are not super convincing out of the gate unless your playgroup is running a ton of Brutes and Monsters. If not, they are probably too expensive for what they do to be truly worth it. I would rather spend the points on something that either does ranged damage or melee damage better than them.

Imperial Ranger Corps

Hundred Kingdoms: Imperial Ranger Corps – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Almost as expensive as the Hunter Cadre, the Imperial Ranger Corps clocks in at 130 points for the first four with 40 points for each further model. They are even more mobile at March 6 with Vanguard, and gain Deft (Re-Rolling Hit Rolls of “6”) while within Zonal Terrain or on top of a Vantage Point.

Their damage potential is great with Barrage (1) at a respectable 14” and Volley 2 with Precise Shot (Hit Rolls can only be Blocked by Defense Rolls that are lower), but a bit unreliable If you don’t gain Deft, since you rely on Rolling hot.

They seem like a pet unit to me. Put in a cheeky little 6 model squad in your Imperial lists and with the proper positioning, good screening and a little luck they can certainly overperform from time to time.

Mercenary Crossbowmen

Hundred Kingdoms: Mercenary Crossbowmen – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Ah, the Mercenary Crossbowmen. They are absolute all-stars in Last Argument of Kings and they don’t disappoint here either. They are a bit cheaper than the Ranger Corps at 120 and 35 per model, and don’t have any of the bells and whistles, but they are rock solid.

The important stats to focus on are Barrage (1) at 12”, Armor Piercing 2 and Volley 2. Everything else, like Defense and Resolve 2, is just gravy. Stick them with a Chapter Mage and you have a great backline unit for ranged support. Not much else to be said here, they are a workhorse unit that will do well in any list that has room to invest a bit into them.

Order of the Ashen Dawn

Hundred Kingdoms: Order of the Ashen Dawn – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Speaking of workhorses: The Ashen Dawn are the scariest thing in the Hundred Kingdoms that is still vaguely person-shaped. They are very expensive. 150 points for just the first one and 175 for each further model, but they do so much work.

March 7 is great, if a little slow for a Cavalry unit, but it is justified by how much of a tank they are: Defense and Resolve 4 with 5 Wounds means that they won’t go down easily.

On the attack, they perform just as well: 4 Attacks at Clash 3 with Cleave 2 and Blessed; Impact 2, Unstoppable and a Special Rule that gives them +1 Cleave on the Charge while Rallying every Warrior within their Command Range (6”).

I can’t say enough good things about them. They are the epitome of the Cavalry Charge that saves the day. Put them to work anywhere and they will win that fight unless they are hilariously outmatched. And the first one is cheap enough to slot into a list that doesn’t want to commit to a full set of three as the heart of their list. Great, great, great unit!

Order of the Crimson Tower

Hundred Kingdoms: Order of the Crimson Tower – Credit: Para Bellum Games

For a budget version, the Order of the Crimson Tower still performs admirably. They have a very similar basic profile, just losing an Attack, a Wound, and a point of Resolve. In return, you gain a Shield, Brutal, Fearless, and a Special Rule that gives them Terrifying when they are Wounded.

Considering that you only pay 115 for the first horse and 125 for each one after that, I’d say the trade-off is worth it, though a single model does not do enough to really consider it in my estimation.

You probably want to run the full three for a true hammer, but if you do, they will output a ton of damage, while grinding well enough to not fall into the Cavalry trap of losing their charge and getting stuck forever.

Order of the Sealed Temple

Hundred Kingdoms: Order of the Sealed Temple – Credit: Para Bellum Games

For an even cheaper option, you have the Order of the Sealed Temple. They clock in at 100 to start and 110 per further model, but they make up for it in mobility, since they are meant to leapfrog from engagement to engagement and sort things out quickly and decisively.

To do that, they are blessed with March 8, Overrun (they get to do a half March when Slaying a Warrior), Impact 2, 3 Attacks at Clash 3, and Flawless Strikes both on their melee profile and their Impact Hits.

Once you have a feeling for their damage and their threat range, the Order of the Sealed Temple will feel borderline unfair for your opponent. Coming in, mopping up, and moving on, turn after turn.

At their current price point, they are probably the best Cavalry unit of the Hundred Kingdoms in the hands of a skilled player, since Flawless Strikes is such a great damage multiplier.

Sicarii

Hundred Kingdoms: Sicarii – Credit: Para Bellum Games

The Sicarii are a strange unit to evaluate. They are basically elite shock troopers: March 6 to get stuck in, and 2 Attacks at Clash 2 with Cleave 1 and Deadly Blades makes short work of most basic Infantry.

On the flipside, though, they are made of paper, suitably enough for a unit that basically goes to war in nothing but a bathrobe. They have a meager Defense and Evasion of 1, but a fairly respectable Resolve of 3 with Oblivious to allow them to test Resolve while Broken!

Last but not least, they get access to Lethal Demise +1 while in Command Range of a Theist Character or Monster.

I’m not sure if I like them at their current cost. 115/30 points is a lot to ask for a unit that can get killed by a lucky hit from something like Militia or Ghols, but their damage potential is very real. I’d say if you’re leaning Theist, give them a try – especially with a Theist Priest, who makes them a Mainstay unit.

Steel Legion

Hundred Kingdoms: Steel Legion – Credit: Para Bellum Games

Finally, we have the Steel Legion, who are probably the biggest chads in the Hundred Kingdoms roster. With Defense and Resolve 3, Iron Discipline and Bravery, they are very tough to put down.

1 Attack at Clash 2 does not seem like much, but Cleave 3 makes up for that in spades – especially so if you can find ways to outnumber your opponent for extra attacks, by running them with a detachment of something cheaper like Men-at-Arms or Militia.

They are a build-around-me unit for sure, and not cheap at 145/40 points, but with the right support they will become a huge problem for your opponent.

Monsters

Gone are the days when the Hundred Kingdoms were the one faction without Monsters. Now, they even have access to two! Though only one really well and truly feels like a Monster. Let’s take a look!

Archangel

Hundred Kingdoms: Archangel – Credit: Para Bellum Games

To the surprise of absolutely no one, the Archangel is the true Monster in the Hundred Kingdoms’ arsenal: a walking cathedral with a truly intimidating statline. March 7 is great for a Monster, but even greater are 6 Attacks at Clash 5, Cleave 3, and Terrifying.

After taking 5 Wounds (a real challenge at Defense 4), its stats dip a bit: March 6 and 5 Attacks at Clash 4 with Cleave 2 – though you do gain Blessed to make up for that.

This is obviously a potent package at 335 points, but a weird balancing factor is Dauntless, forcing you to never decline Duels or leave Enemy’s Engagement Ranges during Repositioning Actions.

And finally, you get the choice between two Aspects:

  • Aspect of the Avenger is the big fuck-off Zweihander version and unsurprisingly aggressive. Here, the Archangel gains Impact 2, Cleave +1, Smite (Enemies hit by its Clash Actions are Broken! and Driven Back), and a Special Rule that forces Enemies to Re-Roll successful Resolve Tests that result from its Impact Hits.
  • Aspect of the Redeemer is the buff wagon spear-and-shield version. With it, the Archangel gains Regeneration (Heal 1 as a Draw Event), Aura of Death 1, Shield (not to be underestimated at a Defense of 4), and the Requiem Special Rule: Enemies that Slay Friendly Theist Warriors within the Archangel’s Command Range (6”) must take a Resolve Test or become Broken!

Both versions deserve equal consideration: The Avenger for a truly heinous Distraction Carnifex that can slot into any list and the Redeemer as a battlefield anchor for your host of Sicarii and Crusaders.

Trebuchet

As if this faction focus wasn’t long enough, we get the wordiest unit in the entire game to play us out. The Trebuchet plays unlike any other model in the game. Strap in.

To start, the Trebuchet has no stats, other than 7 Wounds and Defense 4. It can not move after being deployed, Test Resolve, become Broken or perform any Actions. It brings along four Crew Models that have their own statline – 1s across the board and March 5 – and two Special Rules.

  • All Hands! has you check the Trebuchet for any Wounds during your Command Phase. If there are any, you have to remove one of your Crew models within 1” of the Trebuchet for it to Heal 1. I’m not sure why this implies that the Trebuchet runs on human sacrifice or is made of meat, but we’ve got way more things to cover, so let’s keep going.
  • Take Cover! has any Volley Actions targeting a Crew model performed against the Trebuchet instead, so long as they are within 1” of it.

The whole firing your big artillery piece thing is resolved as a minigame with three important timings: The Command Phase, the Trebuchet’s Activation, and the end of the turn.

  • During the Command Phase, you load the Trebuchet with one of three types of ammunition. We’ll get to those in a bit. Then you put a marker anywhere on the battlefield further than 12” away from the Trebuchet. That is basically where you are aiming your shot. Probably obvious, but note that both you and your opponent know where this marker is during the entire turn.
  • At the end of the turn the Trebuchet fires and you get to apply an effect to the Marker, corresponding to your selected payload. All X values in the ammo types are equal to the number of Unengaged Crew within 1” of the Trebuchet.
    • Stone has all Warriors, both Friendly and Enemy, within 2” of the Marker suffer 2 Hits with the Armor Piercing X Special Rule.
    • Burning Pitch has all Warriors within 3” of the Marker suffer 1 Hit. Any resulting Resolve Tests are rolled with -X Resolve.
    • Explosive has all Warriors within 1” of the Marker suffer 3 Hits. All Warriors that suffer any Hits from this are Driven Back until they are X” from the Marker.
  • When the Trebuchet Activates you get to Activate your Crew models as normal and choose one of the following effects as a Draw Event:

    • Aim has all Warriors suffer an extra Hit from the Trebuchets fire.
    • Adjust lets you replace the Marker anywhere wholl within D6”.
    • Rearm lets you replace the chosen ammo type.

Once you get past the absolute mess of nested Special Rules and visualize the flow chart that Para Bellum should have put in its rules box, the Trebuchet is a really fun model in practice. It’s also surprisingly cheap at 220 points, owing largely to the fact that it doesn’t actually do much in practice, mainly serving as area denial.

Crucially, you want to run the Trebuchet in a list with a ton of activations – so you will likely forgo any of the more expensive Infantry or Cavalry options – since you get the most out of it if you activate it dead last for the maximum amount of flexibility from the Draw Event.

I really like the Trebuchet, but it is anything but user friendly. Even just understanding what it does is a bit of a challenge with how messy its rules are laid out, and there is a lot to keep track of both for you and your opponent.

So like with the Old Dominion’s Fallen Divinity, I would advise any aspiring artillery commanders to bring another list in case your opponent does not want to play the weird side scenario you would otherwise be forcing them into.

Sample Army List

Since the theme of the day seems to be wordiness – and the Hundred Kingdoms are such a fun faction to brew lists for – I will offer you three sample lists here, one for each sub-faction.

Starting us off is a pure Infantry Feudal/Imperial affair:

=== First Blood ===

Feudal/Imperial [1000/1000]

The Hundred Kingdoms

Imperial

* Noble Lord (1) [100]:

* Armsmaster (1) [75]:

* Drillmaster (1) [70]:

* Household Guard (7) [215]: Standard Bearer

* Steel Legion (7) [285]: Standard Bearer

* Militia (7) [100]: Standard Bearer

* Mercenary Crossbowmen (5) [155]:

The idea is simple: Noble Lord and Armsmaster go with the Household Guard while the Steel Legion teams up with the Militia and the Drillmaster for extra attacks, and the Mercenary Crossbowmen offer a bit of reach while your footsloggers advance.

I like the beefy units here, but you could easily cut the Armsmaster for a Chapter Mage with some fiddling, to offer some more oomph to the Crossbowmen. I just really like the Household Guard deathstar so that’s what I went with.

Next up, we have an Orders list:

=== First Blood ===

Orders [995/1000]

The Hundred Kingdoms

Orders

* Priory Commander (1) [140]: Order of the Sealed Temple

* Seasoned Veteran (1) [55]:

* Men-At-Arms (4) [90]: Standard Bearer

* Order of the Sealed Temple (2) [235]: Standard Bearer

* Servite (1) [40]:

* Militia (5) [80]: Standard Bearer

* Order of the Ashen Dawn (2) [355]: Standard Bearer

Pretty self-explanatory. The Seasoned Veteran and Servite bulk out the Men-at-Arms and the Militia, which serve as objective sitters and screens where needed. Your workhorses are the Sealed Temple and Ashen Dawn, the latter of which can also serve as anvils in case you need them.

And finally, there is our Theist list:

=== First Blood ===

Theist [995/1000]

The Hundred Kingdoms

Theist

* Theist Priest (1) [105]: Coat of Arms

* Neophyte (1) [45]:

* Seasoned Veteran (1) [55]:

* Men-At-Arms (7) [150]: Standard Bearer

* Militia (7) [100]: Standard Bearer

* Sicarii (7) [205]:

* Archangel (1) [335]: Aspect of the Redeemer

This is my least fleshed out list, because I don’t really like the Theist units that much, but I wanted to give it a try anyway. The idea is for the Sicarii to be led by the Theist Priest, the Men-at-Arms to go with the Seasoned Veteran and the Neophyte to accompany the Militia.

The Archangel serves as a buff-wagon centerpiece, which can really go with any of our units, since they are all Theist from the Hundred Kingdoms Army Rule. Note that without the Archangel, there really isn’t a lot of punch or durability to any of the other units, so play the list accordingly and keep the Priest close to his walking church to keep it healed up.

Final Thoughts

Whew! There you have it, all the Hundred Kingdoms units in First Blood. The regular humans of Conquest are a joy to look at and build lists for, since they have so many options – with more for the Orders on the horizon, and likely an added Theist expansion in the future somewhere.

If you like list building as much as or more than painting or playing, then the Hundred Kingdoms will keep you busy for a good long while, especially since Para Bellum have done such an admirable job of keeping things internally balanced.

Have fun with your pointy sticks, and don’t forget to bring your Standard Bearers!
