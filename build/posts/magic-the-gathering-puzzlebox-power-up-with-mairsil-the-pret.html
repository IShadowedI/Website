Magic: The Gathering Puzzlebox – Power-Up with Mairsil, the Pretender

September 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer
Marvel Super Heroes wasn't a great set. It could even be said it was a bad set, and in fact, I will—outside of a sparingly few staples for all-but-universal archetypes, and a couple Commanders popular because they appeared in Precons, there wasn't a ton to love about the set. Within its bloat, however, was an incredibly cool Limited mechanic, which helped cards be relevant both in the early & late game: Power-Up. This mechanic allowed you to essentially 'kick' your cards already on the board, either in chunks over multiple turns, or as one larger effect if you had the mana upfront. This played marvelously, pun intended, but hasn't really had a Commander to make use of it in a more powerful eternal format. Not to be dissuaded, after digging around the comprehensive rules for a bit, I stumbled upon a funny little line that changes everything for Power-Up: 702.193b. We'll talk specifics shortly, but suffice to say, Mairsil, the Pretender may well be the hero (or villain) you're looking for, to bring all these strange little Limited cards to bear in Commander. How, exactly? Let's uncage, and find out.

This is part of a shortform Magic series where I’m covering rapid-fire concepts about Commander that I couldn’t cover in a full Commander Focus, so if you like what you see, certainly let us know!

I Got the Power

702.193b says the following: Generic mana in the permanent’s mana cost reduces generic mana in the cost to activate its power-up ability. Colored and colorless mana in the permanent’s mana cost reduces mana of the same type, and any excess reduces that much generic mana (in reference to CR 118.7). Let’s break this down. Every printed Power-Up card has an associated cost with one or more identical colored pips to their mana cost, and some number of generic mana associated with powering up; the idea here, from a design perspective, was for you to be able to play a card like Bold Biochemist in the late game as a slightly larger body that draws, as well as a low Creature on curve as to not get aggroed down. That said, the CR recognizes there’s ways to bequeath these abilities to other Creatures, via things like Mairsil’s Enters trigger, or Agatha’s Soul Cauldron. Any pips a card has that aren’t listed in the Power-Up ‘overflow’ into the generic cost. In those cases, if we cage the Biochemist, Mairsil’s cost of {1}{U}{B}{R} reduces the cost of Biochemist’s Power-Up by an effective {3}{U}, for his one blue pip, and three other cost besides. This compounds with CR 602.2, when we look at a card like Training Grounds—because we can order the way discounts are applied, Grounds’ discount of {2} can cover part of the generic cost (but never reduce it to less than {1}), then Mairsil’s own cost operates with the Power-Up to reduce it by another {3}{U}...to 0. So long as there’s not a double-pip of a single color, Mairsil can usually get down to {0} for his Power-Ups once set up, and boy can we build to exploit that.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

Unlike the other big option for this broad strategy (Experiment Kraj), Mairsil is far better to blink, when it comes down to resetting him for Power-Up; the mechanic does require a given Creature to have entered the battlefield that turn to receive the discount, and given that’s the entire point, Mairsil’s required to enter over & over. We do this primarily via two tools afforded to Grixis, being both cheap blink effects like Splash Portal, and Supernatural Stamina-style effects which prefer we throw Mairsil into the woodchipper. If said woodchipper is Ashnod’s Altar, that can mimic the capabilities of Training Grounds, at least for the first few Power-Ups. With that said, though, Mairsil gets out of hand pretty quickly: Every time he enters, we can exile an appropriate card from our hand or yard, and with a cool nineteen Power-Up pieces to choose from, this Commander acquires a breadth of powers wider than the Super-Skrull, in most incarnations. The fact that Mairsil limits himself to one activation per turn doesn’t really matter with Power-Up, as that’s once per instance of the Creature on the field, and Mairsil already wants to be blinked/returned, both to cage new cards and reset his activation timing. In that sense, it’s a match made in heaven, and solves a major problem with Mairsil as a Commander—there wasn’t really a compelling way to build him that wasn’t just combo, previously. I’ve buried the lede a bit, but Mairsil is a deprecated cEDH darling, having put together a wealth of combos in the past, being largely known for difficulty of disruption once cards were caged; this has fallen out of favor, about a half-decade later, but Mairsil has had issues with popularity since then due to this reputation. Power-Up is mostly just...counters, it’s the ability to get yoked, and this means one of our most compelling wincons is just Commander Damage with this particular take on the card. It’s fundamentally fair, even if the interactions are anything but.

The Great Pretender

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

I think something rather neat about the interaction between 702.193b and 602.2 is that, between Mairsil, Kraj, and Rex, Cyber-Hound, you can technically access any color for perjuring Power-Ups via your Commander. I’d stress that the relative power of those Commanders goes in the same order, with that being the reason Mairsil is the focus of this article, but if you enjoy the gameplay loop of chaining together Power-Ups on a single card there’s a great set of options. Even if you didn’t just want to play Mairsil fairly, you could always toss in a Sage of Hours and put those counters to good use. Alternate extra turns via Kang the Conqueror’s ability and that of the Sage to beat opponents down, just as long as you’re not beholden to the Brackets’ harsh treatment of turns strategies. This kind of thing is exactly what I intended The Puzzlebox to be about, essentially quick bites on how to best-use weird rules interactions, and as far as I can see there’s been essentially no buzz with regards to how transplanting Power-Ups work; this is somewhat understandable, given Marvel Super Heroes hasn’t been terribly popular.

Credit: Wizards of the Coast.

We’ve talked about accessing this via cards in the Command Zone, but that’s not the only way to stuff abilities onto relevant Creatures who might appreciate a Power-Up. Consider this: Hullbreaker Horror & Immortus, Master of Eternity’s Power-Up have the same mana cost. You could conceivably Agatha’s a repeatable Time Twister and mana engine onto control’s best top end, and these aren’t even the best possible use cases. Really, any massive mana value Commander can make use of a transplanted Power-Up, and I’ve been stewing on Lord of the Eagles flexing an Agatha’s-ed Kang or Immortus as a finisher. Important caveat about Agatha’s: While it does allow you to spend mana of any color for activated abilities, that doesn’t just change the costs to Colorless, meaning something like “Power-Up {5}{U}” isn’t just {6}. You can still layer discounts as stated in 602.2, but the ability’s base cost is still reduced strictly by the printed mana cost of its associated Creature, meaning the triple {U} on Kang remains a nagging issue. I felt this important to mention, because if you really wanted to push the power on strategies like this you could conceivably use pairings including Dargo, the Shipwrecker, as an accessible 7 mana value Commander that appreciates a good Agatha’s+Power-Up line. Partner means you have ample access to any color you can dream of, and Dargo does a solid Lord of the Eagles impression. I think there’s honestly room for a lot of Commanders with a high mana value and either a discount and/or counter synergies to slot in some version of Power-Up, because frankly there’s a few of these effects that get out of hand when discounted to a greater extent than their base card allows. I’ll definitely be considering it, when building my next few lists. If there’s one thing I’ve become known for more than putting together Rube Goldberg-esque activated ability strategies, it’s playing with mana value vs actual cost.
