Tabletop Battles Hobby Round-Up: August 2026

September 8th, 2026 via Goonhammer

August is coming to a close and you know what that means - the weather's getting colder, the kids are back to school, and Halloween decorations are somehow already half price at the hardware store. Let's see what our resident crew of 12-foot skeletons have been getting up to!

Jack

Most of this month was spent sloooowly finishing out my second squad of Sanguinary Guard. I converted up a spare banner from the old kit to give me some variety, but I definitely regret it - it took absolutely forever to paint and I don't think it ultimately looks any better than the new one.

Blood Angels Sanguinary Guard. Credit: Jack Hunter

I also, of course, slammed out a few mechs. No particular rhyme or reason here, just some fun units I planned to use in games this month and didn't have done in this color scheme yet.

Dawn Guard Shogun. Credit: Jack Hunter

Dawn Guard Locust. Credit: Jack Hunter

Dawn Guard Vulture Mk IV. Credit: Jack Hunter

Rockfish

Well I've actually had a bit of a light model count wise month, mostly due to being really busy and working on a lot of larger models though admittedly. Kicking off with a bunch of orks...

Gunwagon. Credit: Rockfish

Big Mek Dakkarig. Credit: Rockfish

Wartrakk. Credit: Rockfish

Weirdboy. Credit: Rockfish

Tankbustas. Credit: Rockfish

Big Mek. Credit: Rockfish

Then I did a quick test model for my eldar using a much faster to prime set of base colours, which I am pretty happy with given it took it from like two coats of white primer, a couple of a red and then the orange to get the look before!

Warlock Skyrunner. Credit: Rockfish

Bonus vanguard vets too!

Vanguard Veterans. Credit: Rockfish

SRM

I was about to start this with "It was a light month, but..." only to realize I used that intro last month, and it actually wasn't that light compared to the rest. I guess those months where I crank out 20 mechs for Battletech really skew my perception here.

Ultramarines Assault Intercessors. Credit: SRM

I'm continuing the process of fleshing out my Ultramarines army, buffing up squads from their MSU origins in 8th and 9th edition to unit sizes that can actually take advantage of attached characters and maybe last a round of shooting or two. I had a stupid idea for 10 Assault Intercessors led by Titus and his crew of weirdos for a 17-model deathstar, and needed the appropriate dudes to join him for it. Painting up these 5 Intercessors took maybe a week and a half, with no small amount of time being devoted to their heraldry, following up on that particular decision from like 2020. I weathered their weapons, which I didn't use to do for reasons lost to me, and highlighted their leather pouches and undersuits one stage higher than I did on the first half-squad. It took like 20 minutes (and probably about that much time hemming and hawing) but I touched up their older brothers to match them as well. Maybe I'll do the same as I work on updating this army; we'll see!

Hippogriff AFV. Credit: Campbell "SRM" McLaughlin

Naturally I followed up that last unit by veering completely in another direction and working on something wholly unrelated. I picked up the big Armageddon box full of Imperial Guard vehicles when that dropped, and really had a hankering to paint some regular, realistic-ish military dudes. I tend to struggle motivating myself to paint vehicles, but it would serve as a fun change of pace, and a small enough project that it wouldn't sprawl and take a month or anything. This Hippogriff (named "Honeybun") went together in like an hour and then it was just a process of painting the hull, turret, commander, and wheels in subassemblies. Nothing took particularly long, and I'm so happy with how this cute little gal turned out. It also meant I got to look at loads of reference photos of old armored cars to get the mud on the wheels right, so that was fun.

XCOM Operatives. Credit: SRM

I got to interview Modiphius' Chris Birch about their XCOM game at the end of July, and had been hankering to paint some XCOM troopers since. Fortunately, we got sent the first wave of models to review, and I was more than happy to build them, paint them, and write a bunch of words about them. The Operatives here were a bit more work than I was initially expecting, given their cartoony details, relatively simple designs, and typical poses. Turns out there's a lot of different textures on the basic XCOM Operatives, but there's ample reference material to figure what colors go where. I really liked this group of minis, though would have liked some customization options while building them.

XCOM Wave 1 Aliens. Credit: SRM

Of course, XCOM needs aliens to fight, and I got to paint the Wave 1 set as well. The Sectoids were super fiddly to assemble, but painted up quickly. The Thin Men were less work to build, but took a bit longer to paint, as they've got more detail in the folds of their clothes and on their larger weapons. Lastly, the Outsider probably took longer to research than it took to paint. He's all edges, which I can kind of highlight in my sleep, but tracking down clean reference images was a hassle. Still, I'm glad I got to paint the two sides of this starter set, and hope they'll someday find their way to the table.

September is probably going to be a month with a fair bit of Necromunda action for me. I picked up the new starter set, and I'm going to try to drum up some enthusiasm for it in my local scene. If it doesn't take (or, equally likely, I just forget), then I'll at least have some new stuff to bring to some Necromunda events down the line.

Dandy

It's been a big month for Trench Crusade, with the release of the Carcass Front box and with it, came a bunch of new models that I had the fortune to paint up:

Credit: Dandy

First up are some of the Sacred Affliction models, and these sculpts are just amazing to work with. Lots of mix-and-match options when building them up, and a lot of diversity across the whole range, and even between similar unit types. I tried to go with a similar scheme to my Trench Pilgrims, and overall I am really happy with how they turned out. The myriad of my bandages were a little tricky to work with; after some time, I managed to get them how I wanted. But it wasn't just the official models I painted up this month:

Kitbashed Leper-Knights. Credit: Dandy

Within the Carcass Front box is a piece of Artwork for the Knights of Lazarus Variant (which is also the cover art for the Four Days Dead story), and as soon as I saw this art, I knew I had to try and convert something to represent these towering Leper-Knights. The basis for each model is a Stormcast model, with some 3D-printed bits, Jewelry art, and some coffee to put them all together. I went with regular printer paper to stick on the religious iconography, but in the future I'll try to find something even thinner, or put the paper on before sticking it to the model (I am an impatient person). But these guys weren't the only thing I kitbashed...

Kitbashed Sacred Affliction Anshorite Shrine. Credit: Dandy

Again, based on a piece of art from the Carcass Front box, this Sacred Affliction-themed Shrine Anchorite took me longer to plan than to paint. The bells are 3D-printed, and luckily I managed to find a religious-themed bell to use as the Anchorite's helm. The Millstone and the candles are also 3D-printed, with the rest kitbashed from jewelry art, hobby chain, and bits and bobs from around the house. For the base, I wanted to show off just how destructive these things are, so I printed off a Heretic Trooper fighting for his life and trying to get one last shot off.

Contemptor Kevin Stillman

It's me Contemptor Kevin again, and August was a travel month for me.  I flew from one end of the country and back, and indeed visited more states than I intended to because of a random-ass diversion to Columbus, Ohio.

Before I left on my first big Las Vegas trip, I finished up the Armageddon box Vanguard Veterans.  One of the weird problems/hangups I had with this unit is on the Sergeant: For whatever reason, there's no official pictures from 'Eavy Metal that I could find that showcased the Vanguard Veteran Sergeant with a white-striped red helmet (unlike all the other Ultramarines First Company Sergeants).  So this posed a minor dilemma.  I wound up leaving the Sergeant's helmet white, and using red accents elsewhere on him to denote Sergeant-dom.

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

My two weeks worth of trips meant that it took a fair bit for me to finish five additional Armageddon Ork Boyz, no matter how much fun it is to paint them.  These guys got paint on them in Vegas and then the next weekend when I traveled to Nashville.

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

I was in Vegas for STLV: Trek to the 60th Anniversary, the official unofficial Star Trek convention.  Beyond meeting Jeffrey Combs and dipping my toes back into the waters of Magic: the Gathering with the upcoming Star Trek set (much to RagnarockAngel and FromTheShire's delight, for differing reasons), they also had a painting class there.  I painted the USS Enterprise E.

The last model I finished this month was a mere infantry model, but it is also the final boss of painting Ultramarines.  Roboute Guilliman?  An Astraeus?  A Thunderhawk?  Those were easy compared to this guy:

Credit: Contemptor Kevin Stillman

I decided to do the first of my six Victrix Guard models separately from the others.  As Campbell has noted, each of the Victrix Guard has as much (if not more!) detail than most characters.  And this specific model has enough tiny and different details to make him even more daunting.  I accordingly did him separate.  I'll probably do two more Victrix at a time when I do them (and the Champion separate), just to pace myself.

Boyes

I've had a massive month and a half with my Genestealer cults. I've been grinding away at them and I've still not taken an army picture yet. (mainly because I can't figure out how to get it all in focus)

It's been a character heavy month! The new detachments in 11th really got me inspired to grab a Sanctus, and he's been a total menace at my local even though I think the D3 mortal wound bomb isn't as good as it is hilarious. 

Genestealer Cults Sanctus - Credit Josh Boyes

Credit Josh Boyes

My kelermorph was a bit of a struggle to feel right, and I still don't think I did a great job on the metallics. I can't seem to figure out how to highlight them, especially gold, and make it look good. But he's the absolute GOAT at doing actions and annoying my friends.

Because I reckon I'll be taking multiple of my characters, I gave myself a hard rule of '1' official sculpt, and convert/kitbash the other two. I love how my saboteur turned out, and her little brother even if the hazard marking isn't my best work.  Necromunda hive scum are a truly fantastic kit for getting more out of your GSC bits, I'd highly recommend it.

A GSC classic. Take a mini, put on an extra arm, and boom, genestealer. I picked up a pair of the blackstone fortress rogue psykers, trimmed off the chaos-y bits, and gave him a new staff to act as a second Benefictus. I like how this guy turned out, even if my friends keep saying his scale cloak looks like heinz baked beans.

Space Hulk Genestealers

As a treat, I managed to snag a bunch of Space Hulk 'stealers for pretty cheap, and they make up the chosen alphas of my Patriarch. I added my twist with the face paint, because I liked imagining my cultists getting hissed at as they tried to put it on them, like trying to put a hat on a cat.

And all my mooks to go with it. In total, I've managed to paint 50ish Neophytes and 30 Acolyte Hybrids. It wasn't the slog I thought it'd be, and a lot of it is done to process. I think the colours do a lot of the heavy lifting, I'm really proud of the scheme, I think they come across super punchy on camera and on the table top.

And that's about it! With the new edition of Necromunda, expect to see a lot more underhive weirdos and freaks from me. I've picked up some hangers-on and a Goliath gang to do up, and my Cawdor always have more guys who need to join the fray.

Soggy

Telemon Heavy Dreadnought. Credit - Soggy

I've not been on anywhere near as much a hobby rampage as my colleagues due to real life getting in the way, however did manage to build up a load of 11th edition terrain for the upcoming UK Tabletop Battles Open alongside squeaking in two models this month as part of our review of the latest Adeptus Custodes model releases.

Pallas Grav Attack. Credit - Soggy

I had a few issues Pallas, almost entirely of my own creation which you can read about more in the article. I've recently picked up the rules for 11th edition so am hoping to get a chance to throw some dice and inspire my hobby mojo for September.

Saffgor

With the completion of these Exterminators, I'll have officially finished every single unit available to the Admech, outside of exactly the stilt-boy himself, the Skatros. This has been an absolutely monumental project, and with that army now comfortably available in duplicate or triplicate, I'm excited to hopefully cover them in a full showcase, and move onto the next big project! Compared to my Fusileers, I wanted the Exterminators to err more towards the original kit, because the hunched posture and square silhouette takes super well to weathering, one of my favorite things to do on minis. There's some reposing, especially on the Alphas, in order to break up the overall squatness on display.

Hastarii Exterminators. Credit: Carter Kachmarik.

I'll be taking the leap into Guard now, and I'm excited to do some more work with enamels & water effects, although likely a good bit less patina than with the Admech. Not every commander leaves their soldiers in the acid rain all day, after all. Just most.
